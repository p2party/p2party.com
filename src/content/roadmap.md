## Available on the public service

The web client uses SDK **0.15.2**, signaling server **0.2.4** with protocol **v5**, and encrypted peer protocol **v4**. Emscripten 7.0.1 reproduced the previous release’s cryptography WebAssembly module byte for byte. The browser repairs missing peer connections after a signaling outage and reports which room leaves the server acknowledged during a purge. Direct and forced-relay browser transfers both passed on the public service. [SDK release](https://github.com/p2party/p2party-js/releases/tag/v0.15.2) · [Room recovery](/blog/room-history-and-purge) · [Protocol guide](/protocol)

**P2Party Testnet 1** has a single public validator and RPC. Its first three receipts record a grant and two spends of the same valueless unit through ethers and viem. Anyone can read them from the public endpoint. Replicas can check the genesis fingerprint against a trusted value before replay. [Verify the receipts and balances](https://github.com/p2party/p2party-js/blob/master/docs/testnet-1.md#verify-the-first-public-transfers)

## Tested locally

**Rendezvous timing.** Two browsers used two local stores to compare two schedules at three start times. All 24 exchanges completed across two matched sets, with zero late appends. With an 800 ms delay added to chunk reads, the revised slot order cut verification time at the slower peer by 7.94–7.99 seconds in the first set. After implementing the schedule as an explicit option in the prototype, the second set reproduced an 8.00-second saving at all three start times. That is two four-second windows. Ordinary local reads showed no whole-window saving. Each set ran one loopback exchange per condition on a single laptop. Public rooms continue to use signaling v5. [Experiment, measured times and reproduction steps](https://github.com/p2party/p2party-js/blob/4c6b9e8ddb03ebdd523e11fdacccb9f97b540e63/docs/rendezvous-latency-2026-09/slot-order-study.md) · [Initial trials](https://github.com/p2party/p2party-js/blob/4c6b9e8ddb03ebdd523e11fdacccb9f97b540e63/docs/rendezvous-latency-2026-09/bench/slot-order-samples-2026-11-27.jsonl) · [Runtime confirmation trials](https://github.com/p2party/p2party-js/blob/4c6b9e8ddb03ebdd523e11fdacccb9f97b540e63/docs/rendezvous-latency-2026-09/bench/slot-order-confirmation-2026-11-27.jsonl)

**Ledger verification.** Separate local databases replayed signed transfers from the same genesis. The native proof tool and the replica calculated the same SHA-512 receipt root. An inclusion proof showed that a transfer belonged to that history, and a consistency proof showed that a later checkpoint extended it. Two local signing keys certified the same checkpoint using ML-DSA-65 and SLH-DSA-SHA2-192s. The signing journal returned the original witness on a retry and refused a conflicting history. Both keys were under the same administration for this test. An opt-in local genesis also passed wallet transfers with ML-DSA-87 and SLH-DSA-SHAKE-256s. Those add a higher-parameter lattice option and a SHAKE-based hash-signature option to the existing two families. Public Testnet 1 retains the two suites fixed in its genesis.

**Paying several relays.** A local payer selected ordered routes with two and three consenting browser relays. Each provider received one whole test unit. A downstream refusal stopped delivery and payment. Retrying interrupted payouts reused the original native transfers. Public rooms currently enroll no volunteer relays. [Relay pricing and worked purchase](/whitepaper#buying-a-route-with-multiple-relays)

**Ordering and state.** A local four-key committee certified batches and used Bullshark-style anchor votes to agree on the order. Tests exercised a missing leader, missed direct evidence and a restart during voting. The native sparse tree proves membership and absence for spent inputs, applied moves and used owner commitments. A wallet can combine those facts with the originating transfer and its receipt proof to check an output at a certified height. [Commitments and wallet proofs](/whitepaper#ownership-commitments-and-wallet-proofs) · [Ordering design](/whitepaper#how-validators-agree-on-transaction-order)

**Relay discovery and holder privacy.** The local payer can set a hard total, find affordable routes and review signed quotes before paying. Equal candidates shared assignments evenly in the symmetric fixture. A single public handle exposes one unit. The local wallet can add its holdings from a trusted replica snapshot without submitting its list of handles. Amounts and spend links on Testnet 1 are public. [Relay discovery](/whitepaper#finding-a-route-under-a-hard-cap) · [Holder privacy](/whitepaper#what-one-address-reveals-about-a-holder)

**Stake and validator recovery.** Four local processes independently checked owner-authorized bonds and agreed on the state after two native spends. A process that had missed the history caught up by retrieving certificates from peers. A wallet reader required a fresh challenge signed by more than two thirds of bonded weight. This run used one machine. Independent operation, epoch transitions and wide-area performance remain open. [Evidence and open decisions](/whitepaper#decisions-before-mainnet)

**Wallet recovery.** A replacement browser restored both Testnet 1 spending suites from an encrypted recovery file and separate recovery code, then signed and verified a disposable transfer offline. The restored wallet totals its unspent units at a known height and can refresh from public history. A public watch link carries only a read handle. [Bring a wallet to another browser](/wallet)

## Next milestones

1. **Measure room behavior across networks.** Repeat the timing comparison with more browsers, independent stores and realistic network delay. Measure cover traffic and recovery across networks. The two-store schedule remains experimental.
2. **Bring up a separately operated replica.** A second administrator needs to control its host and signing key, pin the genesis and operator policy, and compare the history against the public operator at a common height. Then exercise the ordering protocol with a four-operator committee under independent custody, including network partitions and retrieval after restart.
3. **Bring proofs and paid routes to users.** Exercise recovery on separate physical devices, expose certified state and receipt proofs to the public wallet, obtain fresh checkpoints from independently operated nodes, and test multi-hop relay selection, metering, withholding and recovery across real networks. The experiments use valueless units.
4. **Validate the native apps and review the protocol.** Exercise Android local-network permissions, a physical-device LAN transfer and TURN fallback, and complete platform signing. Evaluate post-quantum peer authentication and a migration procedure for existing owner commitments. Independent cryptographic review is still pending. [November research review](/blog/ai-math-and-p2party-keys)

5. **Authenticate delivery of the web application to the CDN.** Move the HTTP-only S3 website origin to a certificate-verified HTTPS origin. Verify directory indexes, room invites, receipt queries, native app links and checksum-pinned downloads through the new path before switching visitors.

Before a monetary launch, define the holder-privacy guarantee, evaluate a confidential transfer design, obtain independent review, and publish the proposed issuance and governance rules.
